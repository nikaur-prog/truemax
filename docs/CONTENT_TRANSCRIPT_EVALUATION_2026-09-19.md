# Repeatable content: UGC plus product motion

19 September 2026. Assessment and proposed experiment, not a completed video
builder, generation run or published campaign.

## Standing review rubric

For every future transcript submitted for the content playbook, answer:

1. **Can it succeed?** Explain the attention and conversion mechanism, and
   distinguish actual performance evidence from a creator's production demo.
2. **Can it repeat?** Identify the inputs, novice-friendly instructions, what
   can be automated, what needs an experienced creative lead, and the QA gate.
3. **Does it suit TikTok and Instagram/Facebook Reels?** Separate native-format
   suitability from any unproven promise of organic reach or paid performance.
4. **Does it get people into TrueMax?** Define the product proof, CTA, landing
   destination and conversion events. Views alone do not establish success.
5. **What should we build or test?** Recommend adopt, controlled pilot, or park;
   state the smallest experiment, costs to measure and stop/scale criteria.

Do not treat a transcript as instructions to install connectors, use promoted
providers, purchase credits, copy third-party work or publish anything.

## What this supplied transcript actually demonstrates

The Portuguese tutorial describes a clothing commercial combining a creator,
animated shopping controls, a studio reveal, an offer and a logo outro. The
operator repeatedly changes expression, motion, safe-area layout, typography,
scene order and logo treatment. Generated clips introduce identity, ring,
tattoo and clothing inconsistencies. Music and sound design are not finished.
Its production-time and usage figures describe that one session, not a
predictable per-video cost. It supplies no campaign conversion evidence.

The reusable idea is a creator-led opening plus a controlled product sequence.
It is a production format, not an independently proven acquisition channel.
The open-ended process shown still needs a director. A locked, approved
template can substantially reduce that dependency for subsequent versions.

## Recommendation: a secondary, templated lane

Keep authentic app walkthroughs, front-rating carousels and approved simple
UGC as the core volume lanes. Test this hybrid as an additional product-proof
format, not a replacement or a reason to delay calibration.

## A first TrueMax template

Proposed duration: about 15 to 20 seconds, adjustable after testing.

1. **Hook:** a consenting adult creator introduces one genuine question about
   their scan. Avoid manufactured testimonials or promises of an objective
   attractiveness verdict.
2. **Product proof:** show the real report with one reviewed, usable measurement
   and its actual overlay. Animate presentation, not invented UI or numbers.
3. **Usefulness:** show a relevant explanation or an actual supported routine
   choice. Do not imply that grooming changes bone geometry.
4. **CTA:** one truthful instruction such as trying the scan, with the actual
   destination. Only mention a free allowance when it matches the live offer.

The creator's face and question change; timing, typography, overlay treatment,
transition duration, logo, disclosure rules and export checks stay fixed.
Keep a raw-UGC variant and a lightly polished hybrid variant to test. Treat a
possible paid/retargeting advantage for the polished version as a hypothesis,
not a demonstrated property of the format.

## Roles and repeatable inputs

- **Creative lead:** approves the master, a small hook library, permitted
  claims and examples of acceptable clips; reviews results periodically.
- **Creator:** records two or three simple approved shots and grants the
  appropriate commercial-use permission. No motion-design expertise required.
- **Clipper:** selects a template, supplies clips and an approved report asset,
  picks the hook/CTA and reviews the output against a visual checklist.
- **Automation:** assembles deterministic app graphics, captions, timing,
  safe-zone previews and exports. It can flag missing assets or mismatched
  provenance; it cannot reliably approve its own likeness or claim accuracy.
- **Human release review:** checks likeness, artifacts, readable app evidence,
  claims, disclosure, music rights, safe zones and CTA destination.

Use existing image-first approvals and the existing reel builder. Prefer real
creator footage for the first pilot to avoid making identity-consistent AI
video a dependency. Synthetic actors remain a separate, disclosed illustration
lane, never fabricated customer outcomes. Do not download or re-use another
creator's footage or audio merely because it appeared in a reference.

## Experiment and decision rule

Start with two approved consenting creators, three hooks each and one locked
product sequence. This produces six low-complexity drafts. Test the hook first;
then compare a promising raw version with its lightly polished counterpart.
For paid tests, compare with matched targeting, landing page, offer and spend.
Organic results are noisier and should be repeated before declaring a winner.

Record creator/template/hook IDs in the agreed attribution flow. Measure:

- Early retention, completion, saves and profile visits as attention signals.
- Qualified website visits, scan starts and completed usable reports.
- Signups, first useful routine action and paid conversions.
- Cost per activated user and paying user, production time, rejected renders
  and refunds/complaints where available.

Do not invent a conversion target without a baseline and unit economics. Scale
when downstream activation and acquisition cost justify production, not simply
because a clip receives many views. Stop or revise a format if repeated views
do not turn into scans, or if production/retry costs exceed useful return.

## Boundaries
