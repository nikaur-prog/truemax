# TrueMax AI-actor content lane

## The repeatable reel

1. **Hook (0–2s):** an AI actor looks under-styled, tired or unsure. On-screen: “He followed the wrong glow-up advice.”
2. **Scan (2–6s):** TrueMax face scan and the real exported score reveal.
3. **Direction (6–10s):** highlight two or three specific, non-medical priorities from the plan.
4. **Transformation (10–15s):** show the same AI actor after applying styling, grooming, skincare and presentation changes.
5. **CTA:** “Scan first. Improve what actually matters.”

## Three formats to rotate

- **Transformation story:** before → TrueMax scan → pathway → after.
- **One-feature lesson:** “Why his haircut was fighting his facial proportions.”
- **Expectation vs measurement:** start with a popular glow-up claim, then show what the scan actually measures.

## Production system

- Generate one consistent actor identity with front, side and talking-head reference shots.
- Create three opening conditions for the same identity: poor lighting/styling, neutral baseline and polished outcome.
- Keep the camera, focal length and head angle consistent between before and after shots so the change does not rely on perspective tricks.
- Feed the finished clips into a reusable vertical edit template for captions, TrueMax screen capture, music and CTA.
- Produce three hook variants per concept; change the opening claim and pacing, not merely the file metadata.

## Trust rules

- Label the character **“AI-generated demonstration”** on-screen. Do not present an AI actor as a real customer or testimonial.
  - This rule is scoped to PUBLISHED VIDEO with an AI actor in it — a before/after story a viewer could read as a
    real customer. It does not extend to the product’s own UI. The landing demo card and the gate strip carry no
    on-picture label: they are plainly a product demo, nobody reads them as a testimonial, and the provenance sits
    in the page’s fine print (“Demo faces are AI-generated” in index.html). Do not add the caption back to them.
- Describe the after image as a visualisation of a pathway, not a guaranteed result.
- Do not imply the scan diagnoses medical or dermatological conditions.
- Avoid humiliating “ugly” language. The stronger TrueMax position is clarity and confidence: poorly presented → intentionally presented.
- Separate instant changes (hair, clothing, posture, lighting) from changes that realistically take weeks or months (skin routine, fitness and dental care).

## Hooks worth testing

- “He didn’t need more glow-up advice. He needed the right order.”
- “We scanned his face before changing anything.”
- “Most glow-up advice would focus on the wrong feature first.”
- “This is what changes when the plan is built around your face.”
- “One scan. Three changes. Completely different presentation.”

## Recommended caption

> AI-generated demonstration using a TrueMax analysis workflow. Results and timelines vary. TrueMax provides cosmetic and presentation guidance, not medical diagnosis.
