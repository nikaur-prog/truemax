## Five-second clip recipes to test after still approval

These are proposed original shots inspired by the observed visual patterns, not a request to reproduce any depicted person. Every subject must be an original fictional adult. Start from an approved still with that character's face, clothes and location already established. For before/after pairs, keep the camera, pose, lighting, outfit, background and motion intent matched; only approved presentation differences may vary.

| Recipe | Starting image and camera | One bounded motion across five seconds | Review risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Front portrait | Eye-level phone perspective, chest-up, plain wall or window-lit room; leave headroom for a later editor caption. | First second settles, middle three seconds contain breathing and one natural blink, final second holds. Camera nearly still. | Low relative complexity. Reject face reshaping, repeated blinking, lip movement or exposure drift. |
| Profile hold | A separately approved true side-profile still, head level, visible forehead, nose, chin and ear, shoulders in frame. | Gentle breathing and one blink; keep the profile orientation and camera fixed. | Do not ask a front-facing still to rotate through 90 degrees to create the profile. That introduces unseen geometry and makes identity drift harder to detect. |
| Three-quarter glance | Chest-up against a plain wall, already at a slight three-quarter pose. | A small glance toward the camera, then return to the starting pose. Keep the head turn slight and the frame steady. | Reject changing jaw shape, ear position or asymmetric eyes. This is lifestyle footage, not a geometry measurement. |
| Outdoor walking selfie | Adult character at a safe pedestrian location, eye-level or slightly raised handheld framing, consistent ambient light. | One or two slow steps with restrained arm-held camera drift and background parallax; finish settled. | More complex than a fixed portrait. Reject floating feet if visible, changing accessories, face drift and scene cuts. Avoid intense light flicker. |
| Hair adjustment, optional | Chest-up, one hand already visible below the face, simple background and stable light. | One short adjustment at the hair edge, then hand lowers and the face holds. | Higher risk because fingers can occlude the face. Approve only after the entire clip passes hand, hair and identity review. Do not make this the first pilot. |

Prefer the front portrait and profile hold for the first motion pilot. Keep each generated clip to one action. Assemble a front-to-profile transition as a cut between separately approved shots, not as a synthetic rotation or an in-clip identity transformation.

## How to use these in TrueMax's existing builder

1. Approve stills and paired compositions first. Use lifestyle variety across different pairs, not as an undisclosed difference inside a comparison pair.
2. Animate only the selected approved stills. Review every frame of each returned clip for identity, anatomy, clothing, scene and lighting consistency before acceptance.
3. Hand accepted assets to the existing reel builder. Keep captions, scan illustrations and result cards in the editor layer so they remain legible and do not mutate inside generated footage. The supplied clips provide framing ideas, not data for our score cards.
4. Use a cut or a short neutral transition at the chosen reveal. Do not claim a two-day transformation, fabricate score changes or make a scan-line animation look like proof of a real completed scan.
5. Retain the planned visible synthetic-illustration disclosure in every export. Do not reuse source actors, handles, watermarks, logos, music or testimonials merely because the user supplied the references for review.

The next deliverable remains approved stills and a selected-clip shot list, not a finished recreation of these videos. Camera distance and lighting consistency matter more to a fair comparison than dramatic motion.
